# Research Guide For This Browser

## Goal

The analysis should identify meaningful behavior-combination clusters across gaze, touch, language, referential language, and attention behavior, then ask how those clusters differ between NGT and spoken Dutch, between aud. status groups, across sessions, and around naming events.

## Time-Scale Levers

The window control shows the temporal scales available in the loaded model. The combined model is designed to cover 1, 5, 10, 20, 30, and 60 second windows so we can compare short behavior-building-block dynamics against longer interaction states.

## Behavior-Combination Levers

The most relevant clusters are multimodal bundles, not isolated within-modality codes. Use C0 maps for broad multimodal states, residual profiles for modality-specific deviations inside the same C0, and modality-pair topology maps to inspect behavior combinations directly.

Some behaviors are structurally correlated: mutual gaze depends on child and caregiver gaze, and naming events are a subset of language use. This means code interpretation should emphasize recurring combinations and transitions, not assume that each feature contributes independent evidence.

## Questions To Compare

- Are mutual-gaze-like clusters more common in NGT than spoken Dutch?
- Are attention-getting residuals more common before naming events?
- Do naming-event clusters differ by language modality in their gaze/touch/language composition?
- Do dyads transition through typical broad C0 states on the way to naming?
- Does Session 2 reuse the same broad states as Session 1, or does it change residual modality patterns inside those states?
